Your guest experience questions

The questions B&B owners, tour guides and retreat planners ask me most, in their own words, with the short answer. I'm Linda Covino, an experience designer and Certified Customer Experience Professional (CCXP).

Stays: B&Bs, guesthouses, boutique hotels and rentals

How do I get more direct bookings for my B&B?

Give people a reason to book with you and not with the platform. That reason is rarely a discount. It is a clear story about who your place is for, one thing guests can only get by booking direct (a welcome ritual, a late checkout, a local tip sheet), and a way to stay in touch after they leave so they come back to you next time. Start with the guests who already loved you: they are your cheapest direct bookings.

Guests say they love my place, so why don't they come back or tell their friends?

Because loving a place and remembering it are not the same thing. Guests remember a few moments, mostly the peak and the end of their stay, and forget the rest. If nothing at check in or check out feels like you, the stay blurs into every other one. Design one signature moment and one memorable goodbye, and give guests something easy to share.

How do I get more five star reviews without begging for them?

Reviews follow moments. Guests write about the thing that surprised them, so plan a surprise and put it where it lands best, then ask at the right time, when the good feeling is fresh. Read your existing reviews for the words guests already use about you and build on those.

What should I put in a welcome kit?

Less than you think, and more of you. One local thing guests cannot buy at home, one practical thing that solves the first hour (the wifi, the best coffee nearby, how the shower works), and one personal note. A kit that tells a story about your place gets photographed. A basket of supermarket snacks does not.

How can I charge more than the guesthouse down the road?

Stop competing on the room and start competing on the experience. When guests can compare you line by line, price is all that is left. Add a ritual or a signature moment that only you offer, describe it clearly in your listing, and price it as part of the stay.

Tours, activities and travel agencies

How do I stand out when there are hundreds of tours like mine online?

Most tour listings describe the route. Describe the feeling instead: who the tour is for, the one moment people talk about afterwards, and who is guiding them. Then make sure that moment actually happens every time, so your reviews start repeating it for you.

Go further: Master Your Competition

How do I write a tour description that makes people book?

Write for one kind of guest, not for everyone. Open with what they will feel or see, not the meeting point. Use the words your happiest guests used in their reviews, and end with one clear detail that makes the decision easy, like group size or what is included.

Retreats and events

How do I fill my retreat without spending a fortune on ads?

Sell the transformation, not the schedule. People book a retreat for how they will feel at the end, so show that with stories from past guests and a clear picture of the person it is for. Your past guests are your best channel: give them a reason and an easy way to bring a friend.

AI and competition

How do I get ChatGPT and other AI assistants to recommend my business?

AI assistants recommend the places they can describe with confidence. Make it easy for them: say clearly on your website and listings who you are for, what makes you different and what guests can do, and keep that consistent everywhere. Then ask an assistant to recommend a place like yours and see who it picks. That is your AI shortlist.

Who are my real competitors?

Often not the business next door. Your guest might be choosing between you and a city break, a cruise, or staying home. Look at what your guests could do instead of booking you and you will find where you can really win.
